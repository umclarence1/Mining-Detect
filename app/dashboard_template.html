The labelling problem

Global mine maps miss galamsey

The best free mining dataset was drawn from 2019 imagery and outlines large operations. Training on it alone would teach a model to ignore galamsey.

So the training labels combine those polygons, trimmed to bare ground and water, with a spectral rule: bare soil or muddy water within 300 m of a stream, minus built-up areas. The model then learns the pattern from 900 tiles across the Ankobra, Pra and Offin basins.

Pipeline

How it works

Composite

Google Earth Engine stacks every Sentinel-2 pass from Nov–Feb, removes cloud with Cloud Score+, and takes the median of 12 bands.

Label

Global mine polygons plus a stream-side bare-soil rule give mining / not-mining masks for 1,028 tiles of 2.56 km.

Train

A PyTorch U-Net (ResNet-18) trains on a laptop CPU. Train and test tiles come from separate 10 km blocks, and Tarkwa is held out.

Compare

Both years are mapped, new mining is measured, the area is error-adjusted from a 180-point sample, and sites are ranked.

Limits. The model detects mining disturbance, not legality; the industrial-mine footprint stands in for licence boundaries. Training labels are rule-based, and the 180 validation points were interpreted visually from 10 m imagery by an AI assistant, blind to the model output, without field checks. The area interval is wide.

Data: Copernicus Sentinel-2 (ESA), Cloud Score+ (Google), Maus et al. 2022 mining polygons, MERIT Hydro, ESA WorldCover, WDPA. Area method: Olofsson et al. 2014.
